# Complexity analysis and automatic segmentation

Read during Stage 2 and again if supplied images reveal more complexity than the story suggested.

## Convert the story into visible states

Write a chronological chain such as:

`first cooker on → second plug inserted and both briefly on → overload protector tripped and both off → student reacts`

Create a boundary when any of these independently changes:

- character pose, gaze, expression or interaction state
- prop state such as off/on, closed/open, plugged/unplugged or unlit/flashing
- lighting or powered state
- unstable material state such as no smoke, smoke at source, smoke spreading, smoke at destination
- causal phase such as hazard, protective response and human reaction
- location, time, camera angle, shot size or composition

Internal mechanisms that cannot be seen do not get their own visual state unless the user requests a cutaway or diagram.

## Narrative-time rule

When the premise says a character remembers a past accident, determine what the video must visibly communicate:

- If the accident is the educational content, stage the video in the past incident and show the causal event.
- If the current decision is the content and narration explains the past, remain in the present and show only the decision.
- A time change is a real scene change. Never hide it inside a continuity segment or fake it with a dissolve when the user asked for one continuous scene.

## Split rules

Start another segment when one condition applies:

1. Two primary subjects or systems change state.
2. A prop activates and a person reacts.
3. A precise hand interaction and a high-risk effect compete for attention.
4. A causal sequence has separately verifiable checkpoints.
5. A model must infer both origin and destination of smoke, water, particles or another unstable material.
6. Preventing unwanted but plausible actions would require many negatives.
7. The end frame cannot clearly prove whether the action is complete or still in progress.

Keep a small natural consequence with its cause only when it does not introduce another subject, system or independently testable state.

## Duration

- Low: one simple body or prop movement, no unstable effect — prefer 4 seconds.
- Medium: one primary action with restrained secondary motion — prefer 6 seconds.
- High: precise hands, smoke/liquid, alarm, reflections or difficult landing — prefer 8 seconds and isolate the risk.
- Very high: multiple competing changes — split first. Use 10 seconds only for one isolated action that genuinely needs slow development.

Choose the shortest adequate duration from 4, 6, 8 or 10 seconds for each segment independently.

## Boundary frames

For `S` segments use `S+1` images. A boundary can be:

- a mid-action state when the same physical action continues, or
- a stable causal checkpoint when separating activation, protective trip and reaction.

Each adjacent pair may change only the variables owned by its segment. Freeze every other entry in the continuity bible.
